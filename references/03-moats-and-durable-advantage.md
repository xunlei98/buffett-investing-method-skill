# Buffett moats and durable competitive advantage

## Main idea

Buffett's idea that the best businesses have a lasting barrier, or moat, that protects high returns on capital.

## Why it matters

Part of Buffett's stated method for analysing a company; see [Buffett investing method](00-overview.md). This records what he says he does, not evidence that following it produces returns.

## Explanation or example

See the exact source passages below; the interpretation section restates them in plain English.

## Source claims and exact locations

Buffett's own words, quoted exactly. Locators: ¶N is the Nth paragraph of the converted Markdown text of that letter (see sources.md); use the section heading to find it in the original.

- “A truly great business must have an enduring “moat” that protects excellent returns on invested capital.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 2007 (Buffett letter 2007), Businesses – The Great, the Good and the Gruesome, ¶51, PDF page 5.
- “Such a franchise is usually the key to sustained high returns.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1987 (Buffett letter 1987), Sources of Reported Earnings, ¶22.
- “the best business returns are usually achieved by companies that are doing something quite similar today to what they were doing five or ten years ago.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1987 (Buffett letter 1987), Sources of Reported Earnings, ¶22.
- “Severe change and exceptional returns usually don't mix.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1987 (Buffett letter 1987), Sources of Reported Earnings, ¶21.
- “Companies such as Coca-Cola and Gillette might well be labeled "The Inevitables."” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1996 (Buffett letter 1996), Common Stock Investments, ¶102.
- “The worst sort of business is one that grows rapidly, requires significant capital to engender the growth, and then earns little or no money.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 2007 (Buffett letter 2007), Businesses – The Great, the Good and the Gruesome, ¶65, PDF page 7.

## Interpretation and uncertainty

(Interpretation, not Buffett's words.) He links the moat to returns on capital: the barrier, such as low-cost production or a world-wide brand (2007, same paragraph), is what lets high returns last. He prefers stable industries, where little changes, over fast-changing ones, and he sees fast growth that eats capital without earning on it as the worst case.

These are Buffett's stated principles in Berkshire Hathaway's own publications. They describe his intent and reasoning, not a tested rule set. Verification: every quotation above was checked word for word against the original Berkshire text on 12 October 2026. That confirms the quotations are faithful; it does not prove the principles work.
