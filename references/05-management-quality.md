# Buffett management quality

## Main idea

What Buffett says about the people running a business and the limits of good management.

## Why it matters

Part of Buffett's stated method for analysing a company; see [Buffett investing method](00-overview.md). This records what he says he does, not evidence that following it produces returns.

## Explanation or example

See the exact source passages below; the interpretation section restates them in plain English.

## Source claims and exact locations

Buffett's own words, quoted exactly. Locators: ¶N is the Nth paragraph of the converted Markdown text of that letter (see sources.md); use the section heading to find it in the original.

- “We want the business to be (1) one that we can understand, (2) with favorable long-term prospects, (3) operated by honest and competent people, and (4) available at a very attractive price.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1977 (Buffett letter 1977), Insurance Investments, ¶28.
- “Good jockeys will do well on good horses, but not on broken-down nags.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1989 (Buffett letter 1989), Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years, ¶160.
- “when a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for bad economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1989 (Buffett letter 1989), Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years, ¶161.
- “the overwhelming importance in business of an unseen force that we might call "the institutional imperative."” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1989 (Buffett letter 1989), Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years, ¶164.

## Interpretation and uncertainty

(Interpretation, not Buffett's words.) He wants managers who are both honest and able, but he ranks business economics above management: good managers cannot rescue a bad business. He also warns that the institutional imperative, the pull to imitate peers and follow the institution's momentum, can override rational decisions even by capable managers.

These are Buffett's stated principles in Berkshire Hathaway's own publications. They describe his intent and reasoning, not a tested rule set. Verification: every quotation above was checked word for word against the original Berkshire text on 12 October 2026. That confirms the quotations are faithful; it does not prove the principles work.
