# Buffett margin of safety and Mr. Market

## Main idea

How Buffett treats price, market quotations and the gap between price and value.

## Why it matters

Part of Buffett's stated method for analysing a company; see [Buffett investing method](00-overview.md). This records what he says he does, not evidence that following it produces returns.

## Explanation or example

See the exact source passages below; the interpretation section restates them in plain English.

## Source claims and exact locations

Buffett's own words, quoted exactly. Locators: ¶N is the Nth paragraph of the converted Markdown text of that letter (see sources.md); use the section heading to find it in the original.

- “we insist on a margin of safety in our purchase price.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1992 (Buffett letter 1992), Common Stock Investments, ¶82.
- “We believe this margin-of-safety principle, so strongly emphasized by Ben Graham, to be the cornerstone of investment success.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1992 (Buffett letter 1992), Common Stock Investments, ¶82.
- “we think the very term "value investing" is redundant.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1992 (Buffett letter 1992), Common Stock Investments, ¶74.
- “Mr. Market is there to serve you, not to guide you.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1987 (Buffett letter 1987), Marketable Securities - Permanent Holdings, ¶88.
- “Indeed, if you aren't certain that you understand and can value your business far better than Mr. Market, you don't belong in the game.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1987 (Buffett letter 1987), Marketable Securities - Permanent Holdings, ¶88.
- “an investor will succeed by coupling good business judgment with an ability to insulate his thoughts and behavior from the super-contagious emotions that swirl about the marketplace.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1987 (Buffett letter 1987), Marketable Securities - Permanent Holdings, ¶90.

## Interpretation and uncertainty

(Interpretation, not Buffett's words.) Price matters, but only measured against his own estimate of value: he will not buy if value is only slightly above price. Market prices are offers to use or ignore, not information about value.

These are Buffett's stated principles in Berkshire Hathaway's own publications. They describe his intent and reasoning, not a tested rule set. Verification: every quotation above was checked word for word against the original Berkshire text on 12 October 2026. That confirms the quotations are faithful; it does not prove the principles work.
