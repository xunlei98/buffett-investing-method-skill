# Buffett business-quality checklist

## Main idea

What Buffett says he looks for before buying a business or a stock.

## Why it matters

Part of Buffett's stated method for analysing a company; see [Buffett investing method](00-overview.md). This records what he says he does, not evidence that following it produces returns.

## Explanation or example

See the exact source passages below; the interpretation section restates them in plain English.

## Source claims and exact locations

Buffett's own words, quoted exactly. Locators: ¶N is the Nth paragraph of the converted Markdown text of that letter (see sources.md); use the section heading to find it in the original.

- “We want the business to be (1) one that we can understand, (2) with favorable long-term prospects, (3) operated by honest and competent people, and (4) available at a very attractive price.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1977 (Buffett letter 1977), Insurance Investments, ¶28.
- “We select our marketable equity securities in much the same way we would evaluate a business for acquisition in its entirety.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1977 (Buffett letter 1977), Insurance Investments, ¶28.
- “Your goal as an investor should simply be to purchase, at a rational price, a part interest in an easily-understandable business whose earnings are virtually certain to be materially higher five, ten and twenty years from now.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1996 (Buffett letter 1996), Common Stock Investments, ¶109.
- “Long-term competitive advantage in a stable industry is what we seek in a business.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 2007 (Buffett letter 2007), Businesses – The Great, the Good and the Gruesome, ¶55, PDF page 6.
- “Our preference would be to reach our goal by directly owning a diversified group of businesses that generate cash and consistently earn above-average returns on capital.” — An Owner's Manual (Owner-Related Business Principles) (Buffett Owners Manual), Principle 4, ¶13, PDF page 1.

## Interpretation and uncertainty

(Interpretation, not Buffett's words.) Read together, the checklist has four parts that stay constant from 1977 to 2007: (1) a business he can understand, (2) durable economics, meaning lasting competitive advantage and earnings likely to be higher in five to twenty years, (3) able and honest management, and (4) a sensible price. He treats a share of stock as part ownership of the whole business.

These are Buffett's stated principles in Berkshire Hathaway's own publications. They describe his intent and reasoning, not a tested rule set. Verification: every quotation above was checked word for word against the original Berkshire text on 12 October 2026. That confirms the quotations are faithful; it does not prove the principles work.
