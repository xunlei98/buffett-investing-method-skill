# Sources

All ten sources are Warren E. Buffett's own writing, published free by Berkshire Hathaway Inc. Read them at the official links below; this repository quotes only short, attributed passages. Copyright remains with the author and Berkshire Hathaway.

Locators in the reference files use the letter's section heading plus a paragraph number (¶N) counted in a plain-text conversion of the letter; the section heading is enough to find each passage in the original.

| Source | Published | Official URL | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|---|
| An Owner's Manual (Owner-Related Business Principles) | 1996, updated edition as currently published | <https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/ownman.pdf> | Berkshire's owner-related principles; intrinsic value defined as discounted cash a business can produce; the $1-of-market-value test for retained earnings; preference for businesses earning above-average returns on capital. |
| Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1977 | 1978 (for 1977) | <https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1977.html> | Return on equity, not EPS growth, as the measure of managerial performance; the four-part test (understandable, favourable long-term prospects, honest and competent people, very attractive price). |
| Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1983 (with Appendix: Goodwill and its Amortization) | 1984 (for 1983) | <https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1983.html> | Appendix on economic goodwill: businesses earning far above market rates on tangible assets are worth far more than those assets. |
| Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1986 (with Appendix: Purchase-Price Accounting Adjustments and the "Cash Flow" Fallacy) | 1987 (for 1986) | <https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1986.html> | Appendix defining owner earnings and why they, not GAAP earnings, matter for valuation. |
| Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1987 | 1988 (for 1987) | <https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1987.html> | Durable, slowly changing businesses and fortress-like franchises; Graham's Mr. Market parable. |
| Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1989 | 1990 (for 1989) | <https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1989.html> | 'Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years': cigar butts, wonderful company at a fair price, good jockeys need good horses, the institutional imperative. |
| Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1992 | 1993 (for 1992) | <https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1992.html> | Growth and value 'joined at the hip'; best business reinvests large capital at high returns; margin of safety as the cornerstone. |
| Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1996 | 1997 (for 1996) | <https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1996.html> | Circle of competence; 'The Inevitables' (Coca-Cola, Gillette); the investor's goal statement; ten-year holding test. |
| Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 2007 | 2008 (for 2007) | <https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2007ltr.pdf> | 'Businesses – The Great, the Good and the Gruesome': enduring moats, See's Candies, airlines. |
| Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 2014 (50-year review, with Vice Chairman Munger's letter) | 2015 (for 2014) | <https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2014ltr.pdf> | 50-year review: why cigar-butt investing stopped scaling and how Munger shifted Buffett to wonderful businesses at fair prices. |

## Not included

- "The Superinvestors of Graham-and-Doddsville" (1984). No faithful copy could be retrieved from the publisher, so it is not used.
