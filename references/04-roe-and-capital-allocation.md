# Buffett return on equity and capital allocation

## Main idea

Buffett's yardsticks for judging how well a business and its managers use shareholders' capital.

## Why it matters

Part of Buffett's stated method for analysing a company; see [Buffett investing method](00-overview.md). This records what he says he does, not evidence that following it produces returns.

## Explanation or example

See the exact source passages below; the interpretation section restates them in plain English.

## Source claims and exact locations

Buffett's own words, quoted exactly. Locators: ¶N is the Nth paragraph of the converted Markdown text of that letter (see sources.md); use the section heading to find it in the original.

- “we believe a more appropriate measure of managerial economic performance to be return on equity capital.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1977 (Buffett letter 1977), Opening, ¶6.
- “we find nothing particularly noteworthy in a management performance combining, say, a 10% increase in equity capital and a 5% increase in earnings per share.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1977 (Buffett letter 1977), Opening, ¶5.
- “the best business to own is one that over an extended period can employ large amounts of incremental capital at very high rates of return.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1992 (Buffett letter 1992), Common Stock Investments, ¶80.
- “We test the wisdom of retaining earnings by assessing whether retention, over time, delivers shareholders at least $1 of market value for each $1 retained.” — An Owner's Manual (Owner-Related Business Principles) (Buffett Owners Manual), Principle 9, ¶31, PDF page 3.

## Interpretation and uncertainty

(Interpretation, not Buffett's words.) Rising earnings per share are not enough if they come only from retaining more capital; what matters is the return earned on equity. Retained earnings should create at least a dollar of market value for each dollar kept. The ideal business can reinvest a lot of new capital at high returns, but he notes such businesses are rare and most high-return businesses need little capital.

These are Buffett's stated principles in Berkshire Hathaway's own publications. They describe his intent and reasoning, not a tested rule set. Verification: every quotation above was checked word for word against the original Berkshire text on 12 October 2026. That confirms the quotations are faithful; it does not prove the principles work.
