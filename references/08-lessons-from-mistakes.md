# Buffett lessons from his mistakes

## Main idea

What Buffett says he learned from his own errors, chiefly the shift from cheap, mediocre businesses to wonderful businesses at fair prices.

## Why it matters

Part of Buffett's stated method for analysing a company; see [Buffett investing method](00-overview.md). This records what he says he does, not evidence that following it produces returns.

## Explanation or example

See the exact source passages below; the interpretation section restates them in plain English.

## Source claims and exact locations

Buffett's own words, quoted exactly. Locators: ¶N is the Nth paragraph of the converted Markdown text of that letter (see sources.md); use the section heading to find it in the original.

- “I call this the "cigar butt" approach to investing.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1989 (Buffett letter 1989), Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years, ¶156.
- “It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1989 (Buffett letter 1989), Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years, ¶159.
- “Cigar-butt investing was scalable only to a point.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 2014 (50-year review, with Vice Chairman Munger's letter) (Buffett letter 2014), Berkshire – Past, Present and Future: Charlie Straightens Me Out, ¶279, PDF page 25.
- “Forget what you know about buying fair businesses at wonderful prices; instead, buy wonderful businesses at fair prices.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 2014 (50-year review, with Vice Chairman Munger's letter) (Buffett letter 2014), Berkshire – Past, Present and Future: Charlie Straightens Me Out, ¶284, PDF page 25.
- “when a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for bad economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact.” — Chairman's Letter to Shareholders, 1989 (Buffett letter 1989), Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years, ¶161.

## Interpretation and uncertainty

(Interpretation, not Buffett's words.) Early on he bought statistically cheap businesses with poor economics; he says this works only once and does not scale. Munger moved him to quality at a fair price, the change he credits for Berkshire's later design. 

These are Buffett's stated principles in Berkshire Hathaway's own publications. They describe his intent and reasoning, not a tested rule set. Verification: every quotation above was checked word for word against the original Berkshire text on 12 October 2026. That confirms the quotations are faithful; it does not prove the principles work.
